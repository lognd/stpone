% Chapter 6: the minimum-amount guarantee.
\chapter{The minimum-amount guarantee}\label{ch:guarantee}
\index{guarantee}\index{margin}

The machine is built so that a customer never gets less paper than the label
says. Every wind ends in one of three ways:

\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.5}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.28\textwidth}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}X@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Result} & \textbf{What it promises} \\
\midrule
\shows{** PULL SLACK **}, two long beeps &
  The roll is within the slack (4 inches) of the full length \textbf{plus the
  margin} (margin 0 from the factory). It is short only by the amount shown;
  pull that much paper out by hand and the roll is full length. \\
\shows{DONE}, three short beeps &
  At least the full length plus the margin went past the measuring wheel.
  The roll is good. \\
\shows{!! UNDER TARGET !!} &
  The machine could \textbf{not} confirm the full length. Treat the roll as
  short. Alarm beeps unless you stopped the wind yourself. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

\section{How it works}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item The \textbf{goal} is the target length plus the margin, less the
        \textbf{slack} of 4 inches. With the factory margin of 0, a 30 foot
        roll has a goal of 30 feet less 4 inches.
  \item The motor runs until the paper is one \emph{coast} (8 feet from the
        factory) short of the goal, then it is switched off and the roll glides
        on. The coast is set generously on purpose: running short is fixed by
        the trim, but paper that has gone past the goal cannot be taken back.
  \item After the paper has stopped, the machine compares the count with the
        goal. If it is short, it bumps the motor briefly and checks again. It
        watches how far the roll glides after each stop and sizes the next
        bump so that even three-quarters again that glide stays short of the goal; the last
        bumps are tiny. The bump sizes are used for that roll only. The glide
        of the main run is remembered (until power-off) so that the next ten
        winds cut early enough to stop just \emph{before} the goal (the paper
        can be wound on by hand, never unwound) with no bump at all;
        the eleventh measures it again.
  \item It only reports a good roll when the stopped count is at or past the
        goal: \shows{** PULL SLACK **} if that is still short of the label
        length (pull the slack out by hand), \shows{DONE} if the label length
        was reached. It never guesses: the decision is made on paper that has stopped
        moving.
\end{enumerate}

\section{What the guarantee depends on}
The guarantee is only as good as the measurement. It assumes:
\begin{itemize}
  \item The measuring wheel is \textbf{calibrated} (Chapter~\ref{ch:calibration})
        and its rubber is clean and not worn.
  \item The wheel turns with the paper \textbf{without slipping} and is not
        lifted off the paper.
  \item The paper goes onto the roll; a tear or a jam upstream of the wheel
        will not be noticed while the motor is running (only during the trim).
\end{itemize}

\section{How much over?}
A normal roll is within about 4 inches of the label, usually a little short
(the slack you pull out by hand) or a little over from the glide after the
last bump. When the real coast of your machine is under
about 5 feet the design estimate is well under an inch past the goal on
average and never past the goal; a roll that finishes with bumps is
at most about 3 inches past the goal. If the real coast is longer than the
6 foot setting, the main run itself goes past the goal by the difference,
and the trim cannot take that back. These figures are estimates from the design and have not yet been
measured on the bench (Chapter~\ref{ch:limits}). If your rolls are regularly
much longer than that, recalibrate the coast (Chapter~\ref{ch:calibration}).

\begin{note}[Tip]
The \shows{DONE} screen tells you the exact overrun of every roll, for
example \shows{+4.3 in over}, and \shows{PULL SLACK} the exact shortfall. Glance at it each time: a sudden change is the
first sign of a dirty or slipping wheel.
\end{note}
